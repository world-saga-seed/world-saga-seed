## The page

*The narration: who, what tense, what distance. These are the lines a drafter breaks most often.*

- [Point of view and tense, e.g. "First person, past tense, the narrator's voice; told as it happens."] *(Settled R____-__.)*
- **No hindsight.** [If told as it happens: no "later I learned", "I never saw her again", "for what came after".]
- **The future may shape what is planted, never what is said.** A chapter states only what the cards show on the page at or before it.
- **Never trim a clue.** No trim, cut or revision removes a line listed in `plan/reread-hooks.md` or in a chapter's clue notes. A line that looks like a clue and is listed nowhere is kept and reported.
- **The narrator never lies.** The narration may withhold, give a belief as a person's, or leave the reader to puzzle; it never states a falsehood or a misleading half truth as fact (`story/voice/pov.md`).
- **No explanation of how anything works** [unless your book explains; say which things may be explained, by whom].
- [Spelling: American or British.] [Contractions in narration: allowed, sparing, or never.]
- [Words the page never uses: run the banned list, `tools/tells.py FILE --banned story/voice/banned.txt`, and fix every hit in narration.]
- **Draft plain, do not polish.** No notes, marks or comments inside a prose file.

## Texture

*Numbers from the writer's own pages, measured with `tools/tells.py` on the style samples. A drafter keeps near them; the voice critic flags a chapter well outside them in either direction. The numbers below are only an example of the form.*

- "the way you … / the way a …" simile: no more than [2] per 1,000 words of narration, never twice in a paragraph.
- "and" per sentence: about [0.5 to 0.65]. Break long chains with full stops, not semicolons; do not chop below [0.45].
- No paragraph with three or more negations, or three or more "looked at".
- None of the stock beats: "It was not X. It was Y."; a list of three abstract clauses; the quiet-realization paragraph that ends "and then I let it go"; a summing-up tag line that says what the scene showed.
- A closing phrase used at most once a chapter.

## Length

- **Chapter length is a target, never a cap.** The range in a chapter's notes says how big the scene was meant to be and flags drift (a chapter swelling from retelling, not new matter). A draft well over target is reported to the writer with the reason, never cut to fit.

## Things and places

*The setting's defaults a drafter carries in from elsewhere and gets wrong. One line each, with the card that holds the fact.*

- [e.g. "Light at night is oil lamps; no gas, no electric light." (`world/things/the-lamp.md`)]
- **Check the defaults.** Before drafting, read the list of what is not true here, a plain list in world/, `world/false-assumptions.md`: things a reader assumes that are false in this setting.
- **Before a character invents, ask what the locals already do.** If a trade already has a rule of thumb that does it, the character starts from that habit.
- **Report the setup, not the design.** When someone builds something, the narrator sees what is where and what hands do, never what each part is for (unless they would know).
- **Do not invent staging for a device** beyond what the notes give: show what it does, not how it is fed.
- **Name which one.** Where the page has several kinds of a thing (several boats, several keys), each gets a name the page already uses; nothing new is coined.

## People

- **Give each person only their own habits.** Read the Habits section of each sheet and card for everyone in the chapter. A habit that is not on any sheet is a drafter's habit: log it as an invention.
